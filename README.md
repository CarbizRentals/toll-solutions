# NSW toll recovery — design review

This folder is a developer interview based on a real operational problem. You are given the system as it works today, a change that must be delivered, and two proposed designs. Both designs were generated by AI. Your job is to understand the problem, understand each design, and review them.

Read the documents in the order below. The solutions are easier to judge once the current behaviour and the required outcome are clear.

## How the interview works

1. Read [`current_status.md`](current_status.md) on its own. Build a picture of the data, how a toll notice is matched today, and what must be true after the change.
2. Restate that picture before you open the solutions: what exists now, what is broken or missing, and what “done” looks like.
3. Read [`solution_1.md`](solution_1.md) and [`solution_2.md`](solution_2.md). Treat them as two independent proposals for the same requirement.
4. Review both proposals against the current system and the desired outcome. Ask questions where a document is ambiguous or a decision is missing.

There is no hidden implementation to find in this folder. The review is the work.

## The documents

### 1. `current_status.md` — read this first

This is the brief. It has two parts.

**Current status.** How toll notices, vehicles, bookings, and rental agreements relate, and how a notice is matched to a hire. Use this as the system you must extend. Later designs should fit this model, or say clearly where they change it.

**New requirement.** The problem Carbiz has to solve, and the desired outcome. Use the outcome as the test for every proposal: a digital NSW toll is recorded, matched, and actioned in time; a clear customer toll is charged and the customer is told; exceptions can be followed up; Finance can record, pay, and reconcile what Carbiz owes the toll provider.

Stay on this document until you can explain it without the solutions in front of you. If something in the current model or the outcome is unclear, ask before you review the designs.

### 2. `solution_1.md` and `solution_2.md` — review after the brief

Open these only after step 1. Each file is a full proposal: the shape of the data, the fields that carry customer recovery and the amount owed to the toll operator, and why the author believes it meets the July 2026 outcome.

Read each solution on its own first. For each one, be able to say:

- where a digital toll is stored
- how it is matched to a vehicle, a booking, and a rental agreement
- how a customer is charged, and how a receipt or payment request is produced
- how an exception stays visible
- how Finance sees, pays, and reconciles the operator liability
- what happens to the process that already exists

Then compare them. They solve the same problem with different record structures. Review the consequences of that choice: correctness, operational risk, duplicate or missed charges, and whether Accounts Payable and the tolls team can do their work.

## What to hand back

A written review of both solutions. Cover:

- **Your reading of the brief.** Current matching behaviour, the new constraint, and the outcome you are testing against.
- **Each solution.** What it changes, how a toll moves from import to customer charge and operator payment, and where it relies on the current model.
- **Review.** Gaps against the desired outcome, risks, and assumptions the design leaves open. Support each point from the document.
- **Questions.** Anything you need answered before you would accept or reject a design.
- **Recommendation.** Which proposal you would take forward, or what you would change before choosing. A recommendation is optional until your questions are answered.

Ask questions in the review wherever the brief or a solution does not say enough. A precise question is part of the exercise.

## What we are looking for

We want to see whether you can learn an existing system, hold a business outcome steady, and judge a design that you did not write. Both proposals are inputs. Agreement with either author is unnecessary. Challenge a design when it misses the outcome, collides with current behaviour, or leaves money, matching, or exceptions implicit.
